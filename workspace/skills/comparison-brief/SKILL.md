# Comparison brief

1. Identify the options, what matters to them (check USER.md for their taste, sizes and budget), and whether they named the choices or are still shopping. For named choices, research current facts and decide which you'd pick and when you'd pick another. For open-ended shopping, follow their shopping-link preference instead of forcing a verdict. Finish when the recommendation rests on verified facts.
2. Send one short text with the verdict first and the main exception. Prices, specs and research details stay out of it unless they explain the choice. Finish when they have the useful answer without opening anything.
3. Build the HTML page with the html-pages skill: the recommendation first, then the options with meaningful tradeoffs, verified prices and specs with dates where they change, verified product photos, and links to the original sources or product pages. Finish when the page opens and its details and links work.
4. Save the page in their shared iCloud Drive folder from your setup note, with the files-and-photos skill, and check the file is there. Never substitute a local-only path. Finish when Files can open the final version.
5. Send its iCloud share link as its own message, so it previews: make or retrieve it with Finder's sharing controls and check it points to that file. If you can't make a link (no screen control in this chat, the Mac is locked, iCloud won't make one), send the file itself in the chat and say plainly it's a file, not a link. Never invent a link or say one was sent before it was. Finish when they have the working link or the honestly labeled file.
